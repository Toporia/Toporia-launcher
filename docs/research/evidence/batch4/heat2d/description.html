Research: 2D heat conduction and thermal compliance

Question
What does 2D heat conduction and thermal compliance compute, and what evidence supports its current implementation?

Setup
Domain: 100 x 100 mm plate, 0.5 elements per mm (50 x 50 elements).
Heat sinks (temperature fixed): sink ring at (50, 0), radius 6.
Heat input: uniform over the whole domain.
Material: conductivity 1 for solid, 0.001 for void.
Volume budget: at most 40% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Thermal compliance (default settings).
Method: q4_heat+oc; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
Filters, in order: Density filter (rmin 1.5).
GUI Run is an optimization companion, not a claimed paper reproduction; the saved report records the complete scenario, resolved defaults, field and fresh metrics.

How the function computes
Uses Q4 conduction with distributed generation and prescribed-temperature sinks. A separate strip has an analytic quadratic temperature profile.

Mathematics

A steady conduction solve maps prescribed heat input to temperature; a self-adjoint derivative measures the effect of local conductivity.

Symbols and units
K_k: conductivity matrix; T: nodal temperature; q: integrated heat generation/point heat input; C_T: thermal compliance.

Research target
Gersborg-Hansen et al. heat-conduction examples: exact numbered panel/settings pending.

What is compared
Independent analytic, assembly, gradient and file/metric checks are separate from source-figure reproduction. Histories, fresh final responses and feasibility are recorded without treating a bounded run as convergence.

Limitations
This is a finite-element variant of a finite-volume source paper. The strip is an independent analytic check; the own half-disc-sink example is not a paper figure.
