Research: Vibration through pyMOTO

Question
What does Vibration through pyMOTO compute, and what evidence supports its current implementation?

Setup
Domain: 8 x 1 mm plate, 10 elements per mm (80 x 10 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x and y; right edge fixed in x and y.
Payload: mass 2 spread over (4, 0.5); a frequency objective uses no force, so the load point only marks where the payload sits.
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 50% of the domain may be material.
Objective: Fundamental frequency (maximise) (modes 3, p 16).
Method: pymoto_dynamic+pymoto_mma with attached_mass 2; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
GUI Run is an optimization companion, not a claimed paper reproduction; the saved report records the complete scenario, resolved defaults, field and fresh metrics.

How the function computes
Runs the existing optional-library module network. A fixed-design value/gradient comparison uses Toporia's independent native assembly on the same discrete setup.

Mathematics

Generalized eigenvalues give squared angular frequencies; mass normalization fixes the scale of their sensitivities.

Symbols and units
K/M: stiffness/mass (or conductivity); F/q: applied force/heat; u/T: response; lambda/omega/phi: eigenvalue/frequency/mode.

Research target
Author-library cross-check; original research figure not identified.

What is compared
Independent analytic, assembly, gradient and file/metric checks are separate from source-figure reproduction. Histories, fresh final responses and feasibility are recorded without treating a bounded run as convergence.

Limitations
Agreement between two implementations is not agreement with a published optimized design. Internal pyMOTO filters/mass conventions must be aligned explicitly.
