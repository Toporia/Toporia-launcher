Research: Linearized buckling factors

Question
What does Linearized buckling factors compute, and what evidence supports its current implementation?

Setup
Domain: 60 x 20 mm plate, 1 element per mm (60 x 20 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x and y.
Loads at (60, 10); force 0.1 towards -x (left).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 30% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Buckling load factor (default settings).
Method: q4_buckling+mma; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
Filters, in order: Density filter (rmin 1.5).
GUI Run is an optimization companion, not a claimed paper reproduction; the saved report records the complete scenario, resolved defaults, field and fresh metrics.

How the function computes
Runs the existing Q4 pre-stress and eigenvalue engine, with a separate slender-column Euler check.

Mathematics

Pre-buckling stress supplies geometric stiffness; positive critical load factors are generalized eigenvalues.

Minimizing the inverse-factor norm raises the lowest buckling factors; lambda-star sets the scale and sensitivities include a pre-stress adjoint.

Symbols and units
K_sigma: geometric stiffness; lambda: load multiplier; phi: buckling mode; p: inverse-power aggregation.

Research target
Ferrari-Sigmund-Guest column examples: exact paper panel and source settings pending.

What is compared
Independent analytic, assembly, gradient and file/metric checks are separate from source-figure reproduction. Histories, fresh final responses and feasibility are recorded without treating a bounded run as convergence.

Limitations
Toporia uses an inverse-factor p-norm rather than the source's KS aggregation, plus own column geometry/interpolation. It is not a direct topBuck250 port. Linear buckling omits postbuckling and imperfections.
