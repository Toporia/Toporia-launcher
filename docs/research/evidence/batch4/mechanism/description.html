Research: Compliant force inverter and port adjoint

Question
What does Compliant force inverter and port adjoint compute, and what evidence supports its current implementation?

Setup
Domain: 40 x 40 mm plate, 1 element per mm (40 x 40 elements).
Supports: point (0, 0) fixed in x and y; point (0, 40) fixed in x and y.
Loads at (0, 20); force 0.001 towards +x (right).
Springs: stiffness 0.01 in x at (0, 20); stiffness 0.01 in x at (40, 20).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 40% of the domain may be material.
Objective: Output displacement (maximise) (x 40, y 20, angle 180).
Method: q4_mechanism+mma with move 0.1; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
Filters, in order: Density filter (rmin 1.5).
GUI Run is an optimization companion, not a claimed paper reproduction; the saved report records the complete scenario, resolved defaults, field and fresh metrics.

How the function computes
Optimizes the existing own force inverter. A separate fixed-density reciprocity check swaps input and output load vectors in the same spring-stiffness system.

Mathematics

Ground springs represent input/output ports; minimizing the signed negative output maximizes motion in the requested direction.

The signed port objective requires a separate adjoint, unlike self-adjoint compliance.

Symbols and units
K_s: density-independent port springs; l: output-direction selector; a: adjoint.

Research target
Sigmund force-inverter family: exact paper panel/spring constants not aligned.

What is compared
Independent analytic, assembly, gradient and file/metric checks are separate from source-figure reproduction. Histories, fresh final responses and feasibility are recorded without treating a bounded run as convergence.

Limitations
Own dimensions, loads and springs are not source replication. Point hinges and disconnected thresholded designs remain possible.
