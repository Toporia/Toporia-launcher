Research: Diffuse-interface energy and compliance

Question
What does Diffuse-interface energy and compliance compute, and what evidence supports its current implementation?

Setup
Domain: 60 x 20 mm plate, 1 element per mm (60 x 20 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x; point (60, 0) fixed in y.
Loads at (0, 20); force 1 towards -y (down).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 50% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Phase-field regularised compliance (interface_width 2.5, perimeter_weight 5).
Method: q4+mma; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
GUI Run is an optimization companion, not a claimed paper reproduction; the saved report records the complete scenario, resolved defaults, field and fresh metrics.

How the function computes
Plots a resolved interface and an ordinary regularized-compliance companion using the existing finite-volume gradient energy.

Mathematics

The existing response adds domain-mean Modica-Mortola energy to normalized compliance; w controls the tradeoff.

A resolved planar transition gives an independent interface-energy limit for this double-well convention.

Symbols and units
epsilon: physical interface scale; w: regularization weight; angle brackets: cell mean; C_ref: full-material reference compliance; gamma: energy per unit interface.

Research target
Takezawa et al. Section 2.3 energy family; full numbered source design not reproduced.

What is compared
Independent analytic, assembly, gradient and file/metric checks are separate from source-figure reproduction. Histories, fresh final responses and feasibility are recorded without treating a bounded run as convergence.

Limitations
This is a fixed-load, symmetric double-well response with Toporia optimizers, not a pressure-load or tilted-well Allen-Cahn port.
