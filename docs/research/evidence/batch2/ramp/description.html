Research: RAMP stiffness curves

Question
What does RAMP stiffness curves compute, and which part of its research claim can be reproduced?

Setup
Domain: 60 x 20 mm plate, 1 element per mm (60 x 20 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x; point (60, 0) fixed in y.
Loads at (0, 20); force 1 towards -y (down).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 50% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Compliance (default settings).
Method: q4+oc; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: RAMP (rational) (q 8).
Filters, in order: Density filter (rmin 2.4).
GUI Run is an optimization companion with the setup above; it does not launch the transfer-curve experiment. Figure settings: q 8; the reproduction command saves the diagnostic field or curve with all parameters.

How the function computes
Plot stiffness and slope for q=0,1,5,20 and compare with independent rational evaluations.

Mathematics

Rational penalization has a nonzero slope at void.

The derivative drives physical sensitivities.

Symbols and units
rho: physical density; E: modulus; E0/Emin: solid/soft moduli; q: rational penalty; E prime: density derivative. Superscript T denotes transpose where used.

Research target
Stolpe-Svanberg (2001); original figure/parameter set not available in this source review.

What is compared
The report separates independent numerical checks, equation/curve agreement, paper-visual comparison and unresolved reproduction. Diagnostic success alone never sets a paper match.

Limitations
The q values are declared demonstration choices. Equation agreement is not agreement with a numbered paper figure.
