Research: Geometric beta continuation

Question
What does Geometric beta continuation compute, and which part of its research claim can be reproduced?

Setup
Domain: 60 x 20 mm plate, 1 element per mm (60 x 20 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x; point (60, 0) fixed in y.
Loads at (0, 20); force 1 towards -y (down).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 50% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Compliance (default settings).
Method: q4+mma with move 0.1; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 450 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
Filters, in order: Density filter (rmin 2.4); then Heaviside projection (beta 1, beta_max 128).
Continuation: Multiply by a factor on filters[1].beta (start 1, end 128, factor 2, every 50).
GUI Run is an optimization companion with the setup above; it does not launch the transfer-curve experiment. Figure settings: start 1, end 128, factor 2, every 50; the reproduction command saves the diagnostic field or curve with all parameters.

How the function computes
Plot actual schedule values at completed-iteration boundaries. Compare to an independent clock/event simulation.

Mathematics

The paper doubles beta every 50 steps or at convergence; this clock implements the fixed-interval branch.

Symbols and units
k: completed iterations; beta: projection sharpness. Values double after each 50 completed steps and stop at 128. Superscript T denotes transpose where used.

Research target
Wang et al. (2011), section 4 continuation accompanying Figures 3-4.

What is compared
The report separates independent numerical checks, equation/curve agreement, paper-visual comparison and unresolved reproduction. Diagnostic success alone never sets a paper match.

Limitations
A schedule plot verifies the fixed-interval rule only. It omits early increases at convergence and does not reproduce an optimized paper design.
