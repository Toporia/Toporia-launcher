Research: Mirror symmetry

Question
What does Mirror symmetry compute, and which part of its research claim can be reproduced?

Setup
Domain: 60 x 30 mm plate, 1 element per mm (60 x 30 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x and y.
Loads at (60, 15); force 1 towards -y (down).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 40% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Compliance (default settings).
Method: q4+mma with move 0.1; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
Filters, in order: Density filter (rmin 2.4); then Symmetry filter (axis bottom_top).
GUI Run is an optimization companion with the setup above; it does not launch the transfer-curve experiment. Figure settings: axis both; the reproduction command saves the diagnostic field or curve with all parameters.

How the function computes
Show an asymmetric field before and after mirror averaging; independently check symmetry, mean and idempotence.

Mathematics

Mirror averaging is an orthogonal projection; both axes compose two commuting projections.

Symbols and units
x: input field; M: reflection operator; I: identity; P: averaging projection. Both-axis symmetry composes horizontal and vertical averages. Superscript T denotes transpose where used.

Research target
Standard symmetry restriction; no specific book figure assigned to this Toporia implementation.

What is compared
The report separates independent numerical checks, equation/curve agreement, paper-visual comparison and unresolved reproduction. Diagnostic success alone never sets a paper match.

Limitations
This is an engineering operator demonstration. Symmetric density is not the same as symmetric loads or a symmetric state solution.
