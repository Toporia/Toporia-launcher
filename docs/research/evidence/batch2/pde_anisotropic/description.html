Research: Directional PDE filter

Question
What does Directional PDE filter compute, and which part of its research claim can be reproduced?

Setup
Domain: 60 x 20 mm plate, 1 element per mm (60 x 20 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x; point (60, 0) fixed in y.
Loads at (0, 20); force 1 towards -y (down).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 50% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Compliance (default settings).
Method: q4+oc; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
Filters, in order: Anisotropic PDE (Helmholtz) filter (rmin_x 6, rmin_y 2).
GUI Run is an optimization companion with the setup above; it does not launch the transfer-curve experiment. Figure settings: rmin_x 6, rmin_y 2; the reproduction command saves the diagnostic field or curve with all parameters.

How the function computes
Solve a cell-centred directional Helmholtz equation. Compare a cosine mode against its analytic Neumann solution.

Mathematics

Axis-aligned finite-volume diffusion; different radii smooth directions differently.

A symmetric no-flux matrix preserves constants and the summed field.

Symbols and units
x: source density; rho-bar: smoothed density; D: diagonal diffusion tensor; r_a: radius along axis a in cells; A: finite-volume matrix; J: scalar response. Superscript T denotes transpose where used.

Research target
Lazarov-Sigmund Helmholtz formulation; no matching directional finite-volume paper panel identified.

What is compared
The report separates independent numerical checks, equation/curve agreement, paper-visual comparison and unresolved reproduction. Diagnostic success alone never sets a paper match.

Limitations
Toporia's anisotropic cell-centred extension differs from the paper's nodal discretization. The generated impulse/cosine figure is an analytic verification, not a reproduced research panel.
