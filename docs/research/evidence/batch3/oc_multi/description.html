Research: OC with several multipliers

Question
How does OC with several multipliers turn a design and sensitivities into the next material field?

Setup
Domain: 60 x 20 mm plate, 1 element per mm (60 x 20 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x; point (60, 0) fixed in y.
Loads at (0, 20); force 1 towards -y (down).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 50% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Compliance (default settings).
Constraints: Peak von Mises stress (limit 0.5).
Method: q4+oc_multi; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
Filters, in order: Density filter (rmin 1.5).
GUI Run is an optimization companion, not a claimed paper reproduction; the full numerical evidence is recorded separately.

How the function computes
Uses one volume and one aggregate stress constraint in the companion, so the extra multiplier path is exercised.

Mathematics

Cyclic multiplier searches generalize the multiplicative OC rule; safeguards handle gradient signs.

Symbols and units
z: design variables; rho: physical element density; C or f: objective; g: normalized inequality, feasible when <=0; J: Jacobian; superscript T: transpose; plus: next iterate; ell/h: move-and-box bounds. K: stiffness; u: displacement; F: applied load; N: cell count. Coordinates use the scenario's mm units; density is dimensionless. d: objective direction; v_ij: gradient of constraint i; lambda_i: multiplier; eta: damping exponent.

Research target
Zhou-Rozvany (1991) COC examples: panel/inputs pending.

What is compared
The experiment records initial/final fields, pre-update objective histories, fresh final compliance, actual physical volume and solve counts. Independent operator checks and source reviews are reported separately. All unmatched paper figures remain in to-fix.

Limitations
Toporia's cyclic-bisection variant does not establish feasibility or convergence of general signed-gradient constraints.
