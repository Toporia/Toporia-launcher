Research: Geometry projection capsule bars

Question
How does Geometry projection capsule bars turn a design and sensitivities into the next material field?

Setup
Domain: 60 x 20 mm plate, 1 element per mm (60 x 20 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x; point (60, 0) fixed in y.
Loads at (0, 20); force 1 towards -y (down).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 50% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Compliance (default settings).
Method: q4+mma with move 0.02; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Geometry projection (capsule bars) (n_x 3, n_y 2); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
Filters, in order: Density filter (rmin 1.5).
GUI Run is an optimization companion, not a claimed paper reproduction; the full numerical evidence is recorded separately.

How the function computes
Projects floating fixed-radius capsule bars with activation variables, then combines them through a bounded product union.

Mathematics

Distance to a capsule medial segment defines an inside-positive boundary distance normalized by the sampling window.

A locally flat interface cuts a disk or ball; these are its area/volume fractions, independently checked by quadrature.

Toporia joins components by a bounded product union and carries sensitivities through its Jacobian. This differs from a maximum or p-norm union.

Symbols and units
z: design variables; rho: physical element density; C or f: objective; g: normalized inequality, feasible when <=0; J: Jacobian; superscript T: transpose; plus: next iterate; ell/h: move-and-box bounds. K: stiffness; u: displacement; F: applied load; N: cell count. Coordinates use the scenario's mm units; density is dimensionless. a/b: segment endpoints; R: fixed capsule radius; r: sampling radius; t: normalized signed distance; H2/H3: 2D/3D cap fractions; alpha: activation multiplying each component.

Research target
Smith-Norato (2020), Figure 1 geometry and equations 1-3: cap construction; optimized examples not reproduced.

What is compared
The experiment records initial/final fields, pre-update objective histories, fresh final compliance, actual physical volume and solve counts. Independent operator checks and source reviews are reported separately. All unmatched paper figures remain in to-fix.

Limitations
The cap-integral comparison checks the construction with explicit inside-positive signs. Published HTML equation/sign conventions need care. Fixed radius, product union and separately penalized combined density differ from GPTO.
