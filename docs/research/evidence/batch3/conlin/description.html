Research: CONLIN mixed variables

Question
How does CONLIN mixed variables turn a design and sensitivities into the next material field?

Setup
Domain: 60 x 20 mm plate, 1 element per mm (60 x 20 elements).
Supports: left edge fixed in x; point (60, 0) fixed in y.
Loads at (0, 20); force 1 towards -y (down).
Material: Young's modulus 1 for solid, 1e-09 for void, Poisson ratio 0.3.
Volume budget: at most 50% of the domain may be material.
Objective: minimise Compliance (default settings).
Method: q4+conlin; stops when the design changes by less than 0.01, at most 300 iterations.
Design variables: Element densities (default settings); material law: SIMP (power law) (default settings).
Filters, in order: Density filter (rmin 1.5).
GUI Run is an optimization companion, not a claimed paper reproduction; the full numerical evidence is recorded separately.

How the function computes
Builds mixed-variable objective/constraint approximations and solves their multiplier dual with L-BFGS-B.

Mathematics

Positive derivatives use direct variables; negative derivatives use reciprocals, giving a separable convex approximation.

Symbols and units
z: design variables; rho: physical element density; C or f: objective; g: normalized inequality, feasible when <=0; J: Jacobian; superscript T: transpose; plus: next iterate; ell/h: move-and-box bounds. K: stiffness; u: displacement; F: applied load; N: cell count. Coordinates use the scenario's mm units; density is dimensionless. g_j: derivative at the current point; k: iteration.

Research target
Fleury-Braibant (1986) numerical example: panel/inputs pending.

What is compared
The experiment records initial/final fields, pre-update objective histories, fresh final compliance, actual physical volume and solve counts. Independent operator checks and source reviews are reported separately. All unmatched paper figures remain in to-fix.

Limitations
Own implementation with move limits; no published CONLIN trajectory is used as a reference.
