Research: BESO hard kill

Question
How does BESO hard kill turn a design and sensitivities into the next material field?

Setup
GUI Run is a bounded optimization companion, not a claimed paper reproduction. Half-MBB domain 60 by 20 mm, mesh 30 by 10, fraction 0.5, E0=1, Emin=1e-9, nu=0.3, top-left downward unit load, left horizontal symmetry and bottom-right vertical roller. Method q4+beso_hard; cap 120; tol=0 (cap or optimizer's own stop). Representation {'type': 'element_density', 'start': 'full'}; parameters {}; constraints []; filters [{'type': 'density', 'rmin': 1.5}]. Full numerical evidence is separate from this recipe.

How the function computes
Starts fully solid, evolves a binary design and uses objective-window convergence rather than maximum element change.

Mathematics

Reduce the allowed material gradually and rank filtered/history-smoothed sensitivities, with a cap on additions.

Symbols and units
z: design variables; rho: physical element density; C or f: objective; g: normalized inequality, feasible when <=0; J: Jacobian; superscript T: transpose; plus: next iterate; ell/h: move-and-box bounds. K: stiffness; u: displacement; F: applied load; N: cell count. Coordinates use the scenario's mm units; density is dimensionless. V: material budget; V*: final budget; ER: evolutionary removal fraction; z_min: soft or hard void bound.

Research target
Huang-Xie BESO beam examples: exact panel and history pending.

What is compared
The experiment records initial/final fields, pre-update objective histories, fresh final compliance, actual physical volume and solve counts. Independent operator checks and source reviews are reported separately. All unmatched paper figures remain in to-fix.

Limitations
Hard kill uses exact design void bounds but the physics retains ersatz stiffness. Sensitivity histories, additions and discrete ties matter.
